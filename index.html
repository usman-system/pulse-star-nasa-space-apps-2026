Evaluating Under-Characterized Terrestrial Analogs Against Established Lunar & Martian Benchmarks

No location on Earth perfectly reproduces the reduced gravity, atmospheric pressure, or radiation environment of the Moon or Mars. Pulse Star provides a transparent, evidence-traceable research workflow that evaluates one under-characterized Provisional Candidate—Chandragup I Mud Volcano in Hingol National Park, Balochistan, Pakistan—against five established Literature-Backed Reference Benchmarks (two lunar-base benchmarks and three Martian landing/exploration benchmarks) across five independent qualitative rubric dimensions (morphology, geochemistry, eva_trafficability, thermal_regime, and subsurface_potential).

Primary Candidate Study • Provisional Status WGS84: 25.4453°N, 65.8656°E (Hingol National Park, Pakistan)

Chandragup I Mud Volcano ↔ Acidalia Planitia Pitted Cones (Proposed Comparative Hypothesis)

  • Proposed Comparative Hypothesis: Pairing Chandragup I with the ~18,000 circular-to-subcircular pitted cones in southern Acidalia Planitia on Mars (49.80°N, 339.30°E; Oehler & Allen, 2010) is a provisional comparative hypothesis synthesizing terrestrial Makran studies with Martian orbital interpretations. Oehler & Allen (2010) analyze Martian landforms and do not study or cite Chandragup I.
  • Site-Specific Chandragup I Evidence: Published field surveys (Delisle et al., 2002) directly document Chandragup I's alignment along the SW/NE-trending Dhak Anticline and near-ambient crater-lake mud temperatures rising slightly (~1.5°C) during episodes of modest mud outflow. Satellite and structural mapping (Kassi et al., 2014) distinguishes the main Chandragup cluster (2–8 km SE of the Hingol River: active Chandragup I, water-filled South Chandragup-II 2.5 m below rim, and 30-m dried-up Chandragup-III) from the newly identified South Chandragup subcluster (~18 km away in the Dhak Desert).
  • Regional Makran vs. Unverified Single-Vent Data: Bacterial methane (CH4) gas origin, 2–3 km mixed benthic foraminiferal and calcareous nannofloral mud-source depths, and discharge ranges (0–1.4 m³/h mud; negligible to 1 m³/s gas) in Delisle et al. (2002) are reported across the studied Dhak Anticline / eastern Makran mud-volcano cluster; Chandragup I-specific methane emission rates, isotopic ratios (δ13C, δD), and single-vent discharge table entries require subscription full-text access and have not been independently verified in this application.

Physical Boundary Offsets & Unmeasured Parameters

  • Surface Gravity & Terramechanical Boundary (9.81 m/s² Earth vs. 3.71 m/s² Mars; Ratio ~0.378): Mars surface gravity is approximately 3.71 m/s², or 0.378 times Earth’s gravity. Terrestrial trafficability trials conducted under Earth’s gravity cannot directly reproduce Martian wheel-soil interaction. Any extrapolation must account for differences in normal loading, traction, soil cohesion, internal friction, vehicle mass distribution and suspension dynamics. A simple 0.378 multiplier is not a validated scaling model.
  • Atmospheric Pressure Breakdown: Earth atmospheric pressure (~1013 mbar N₂/O₂) differs fundamentally from Mars (~6.1 mbar CO₂), where extruded water-mud slurries undergo instantaneous evaporative boiling and surface freezing (Broz et al., 2020).
  • Mineralogical Offset: Chandragup erupts terrigenous quartz-illite-chlorite marine shale slurry from the Makran subduction zone, whereas Acidalia Planitia sediments derive from altered basaltic/ferroan smectite highland outflow detritus.
  • Unmeasured Parameters Preserved as null: Mean annual temperature, mud slurry pH, and diurnal thermal swing at Chandragup I are unmeasured in the examined sources and preserved strictly as null (Unknown / Not Measured), never converted to 0.
  • Dataset Transparency: Landsat 8/9 OLI/TIRS and ASTER coverage at Chandragup I are literature-referenced and basemap-inspected; raw NASA/ESA PDS spectral granules were not independently processed in-app.

      How Qualitative Triage Ratings (0–100 Scale) & Evidence Statuses Are Assigned

      Assessment ratings are qualitative, literature-informed triage judgements. They are not measured physical similarity values, validated predictive outputs, or independently calculated scientific measurements.

      Not Measured Percentages • Zero Composite Averaging • Null Never Treated as Zero
      1. Nature of Rubric Ratings

      Ratings (0–100) are qualitative ordinal comparative indices assigned from published literature, not measured physical similarity percentages. Each dimension is evaluated independently across: (a) shared geological/geochemical mechanisms, (b) published comparative studies, (c) documented agency field trials, and (d) physical boundary offsets (gravity, pressure, radiation, temperature).

      Rule: Pulse Star never computes an overall composite score and never converts unmeasured (null) values to 0.

      2. Three Evidence Classifications
      Literature-Backed: Supported by published peer-reviewed planetary analog studies or agency field campaigns for that dimension.
      Provisional: Proposed comparative hypothesis inferred from regional geological surveys and orbital geomorphology; awaits dedicated field validation.
      Unassessed / Unknown: Insufficient empirical evidence; stored as null and never converted to 0.
      3. Qualitative Ordinal Scale Bands
      • 90–100 (Benchmark): Multi-parameter validation in peer-reviewed astrobiology, geochemistry, or rover/EVA field campaigns.
      • 75–89 (High Process Match): Strong mechanistic or structural analogy for target landforms, with documented ambient offsets.
      • 50–74 (Partial / Single-Axis): Useful for isolated engineering/sensor tests, but major divergence in ambient environment.
      • 0–49 (Low Correspondence): Superficial visual similarity without shared formation mechanism.
